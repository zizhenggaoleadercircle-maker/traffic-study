## 1. City of Toronto - Vision Zero (direct target)

**Plan:** Vision Zero 2.0 Road Safety Plan, adopted July 2019; data-driven and map-based decision-making.

**Seven emphasis areas** (official user segments Toronto prioritizes):

1. Pedestrians
2. School-aged children (4-19)
3. Older adults
4. Cyclists
5. Motorcyclists
6. Aggressive driving and distraction
7. Heavy trucks

**2026 Council actions (March 2026):**

- New Crosswalk Pavement Marking Policy and All-Way Stop Sign Control Justification Policy
- Expanded Community Safety Zones outside school zones (no longer gated on Automated Speed Enforcement feasibility)
- Missing Sidewalk Installations authorized on 15 street segments
- Pedestrian crossing prohibitions at two specific intersections

**Current performance (as of May 2026):** 15 fatalities and 40 serious injuries year-to-date; pedestrians are the majority of fatalities (11 of 15). Toronto publishes a public Vision Zero Dashboard and KSI data on Toronto Police Service Open Data and Toronto Open Data.

**Toolkit they actually deploy:** speed reductions, shortened crossing distances, no-right-turns on red, advance pedestrian signals, protected left-turns, school zone safety reviews within 1 km of child KSI events.

> Implication for this project: if you want planner buy-in, frame outputs around these seven emphasis areas (especially pedestrians and cyclists), and use KSI as the canonical outcome metric. That is the metric they report to Council.

Sources: [Vision Zero Plan Overview](https://www.toronto.ca/services-payments/streets-parking-transportation/road-safety/vision-zero/vision-zero-plan-overview/), [Emphasis Areas](https://www.toronto.ca/services-payments/streets-parking-transportation/road-safety/vision-zero/emphasis-areas/), [Vision Zero Dashboard](https://www.toronto.ca/services-payments/streets-parking-transportation/road-safety/vision-zero/vision-zero-dashboard/), [Council Agenda 2026.IE27.9](https://secure.toronto.ca/council/agenda-item.do?item=2026.IE27.9).

## 2. Vision Zero Network (US national coordinator)

**2026 strategic posture:** "Double down on what works." The network is past the launch phase and is now focused on scaling proven interventions and sustaining political momentum.

**Proven results they are amplifying (2025-2026):**

- Columbus: 6-lane to 4-lane corridor with protected bike lanes; 54% crash reduction, 89% drop in excessive speeding
- Detroit: ~2,000 speed humps annually; 22% reduction in residential street crashes (2020-2022)
- Fort Lauderdale: traffic calming dropped share of speeders from 60% to 1% northbound
- City-level 2025 fatality declines: San Francisco 42%, Portland 32%, Chicago 23%, New York City 18%

**Methodology priorities:**

- High Injury Network (HIN) prioritization: focus interventions on the small share of roads where most KSI occurs
- Equity overlay: combine HIN with demographic data (low-income, minority, LEP, disabled, youth, elderly)
- Upstream and preventive interventions, not reactive enforcement

Sources: [Vision Zero in 2026](https://visionzeronetwork.org/vision-zero-in-2026-why-the-next-phase-matters-more-than-ever/), [National Priorities](https://visionzeronetwork.org/national-priorities/), [Holistic Prioritization](https://visionzeronetwork.org/prioritizing-for-greatest-impact/).

## 3. USDOT - National Roadway Safety Strategy (NRSS)

**Frame:** Safe System Approach, organized around five objectives:

1. Safer People
2. Safer Roads
3. Safer Vehicles
4. Safer Speeds
5. Post-Crash Care

**Equity is an explicit pillar.** Recent NRSS actions:

- Safe Streets and Roads for All (SS4A) discretionary grant program: $2.9B+ to 1,600+ communities (~75% of US population)
- Reconnecting Communities Program (45 projects) addressing historical infrastructure harms
- Thriving Communities Program: technical assistance for 64 under-resourced communities

Sources: [NRSS](https://www.transportation.gov/NRSS), [Safe System Approach](http://www.transportation.gov/NRSS/SafeSystem), [NRSS Progress Report](https://www.transportation.gov/briefing-room/usdot-publishes-national-roadway-safety-strategy-progress-report-welcomes-new-allies).

## 4. FHWA - Safe System Approach (canonical framework)

**Five elements:**

1. Safe Roads: forgiving infrastructure
2. Safe Speeds: speed management
3. Safe Vehicles: vehicle design and technology
4. Safe Road Users: behavior and education
5. Post-Crash Care: emergency response

**Six core principles** (suitable for direct quotation in a briefing PDF):

- Deaths and serious injuries are unacceptable
- Humans make mistakes; the system must be forgiving
- Humans are vulnerable; bodies have crash-tolerance limits
- Responsibility is shared
- Safety is proactive
- Redundancy is crucial

**FHWA pedestrian and cyclist priorities:**

- Improve roadway design and operations for vulnerable road users
- Manage vehicle speeds
- Build tools to assess safety impacts (this is essentially what this project does)
- Leverage emerging technology
- Equity in safety, comfort, and mobility
- Safe, accessible, connected multimodal networks

Sources: [Safe System Primer for Pedestrians and Bicyclists](https://highways.dot.gov/safety/pedestrian-bicyclist/safe-system/primer-safe-system-approach-pedestrians-and-bicyclists), [Safe System Approach](https://highways.dot.gov/safety/zero-deaths/safe-system-approach-toward-zero-traffic-deaths).

## 5. NACTO (National Association of City Transportation Officials)

**Vision Zero advocacy priorities:**

- Reform speed limits via multimodal street design and automated enforcement
- Vehicle design standards protecting people outside cars (visibility, large-vehicle features)
- Modernize MUTCD; remove design restrictions that block safety upgrades
- Re-evaluate traffic laws that drive over-policing without safety benefit

**Federal policy platform:**

- Complete Streets first; safety for all users
- Flexible city-level transportation funding
- Prioritize transit
- Fund sidewalks, crossing islands, bike lanes, road reconfigurations
- Require states to set annual fatality-reduction goals

Sources: [NACTO Vision Zero](https://nacto.org/topic/vision-zero/), [NACTO Federal Policy Platform](https://nacto.org/publication/federal-policy-platform/).

## 6. WHO - Decade of Action for Road Safety 2021-2030

**Target:** at least 50% reduction in road traffic deaths and injuries by 2030.

**Five action areas** (pillar 5 is uniquely WHO):

1. Safe road infrastructure
2. Safe vehicles
3. Safe road use
4. Post-crash response
5. Multimodal transport and land-use planning

**Cross-cutting priorities:** speed management, legal frameworks, capacity development, gender, technology, financing, and accelerating safe walking, cycling, and transit.

Sources: [WHO Decade of Action launch](https://www.who.int/news/item/28-10-2021-who-kicks-off-a-decade-of-action-for-road-safety), [Global Plan PDF](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/documents/health-topics/road-traffic-injuries/global-plan-for-road-safety.pdf).

## 7. International - Towards Zero Foundation and iRAP

**Towards Zero Foundation (UK charity, global reach):**

- Endorses Safe System approach
- Runs Global NCAP, Stop the Crash Partnership, Commonwealth Road Safety Initiative
- "50 by 30" campaign: halve global road deaths by 2030

**iRAP (International Road Assessment Programme):**

- Methodology in 130+ countries, ~3 million km of roads assessed
- Influenced $110B+ in road projects; ~860,000 deaths and serious injuries prevented (cumulative through 2024)
- Star Rating system: segments rated 1-5 stars; 3-star or better is the safety target
- AiRAP: AI/ML-driven road safety data capture (directly relevant to the ML predictor framing in this project)

Sources: [Towards Zero Foundation](https://www.towardszerofoundation.org/our-organisation), [iRAP What We Do](https://irap.org/what-we-do/).

## 8. Methodology standard: the High Injury Network (HIN)

If presenting to Toronto Transportation Services, this is the methodology language they recognize.

- **Inputs:** police crash data, plus hospital injury data where available, plus road network inventory
- **Window:** typically 3-5 years of crash history
- **Sliding window:** crashes assigned to overlapping segments (~1-mile windows, shifted incrementally) to smooth geocoding error
- **Ranking:** segments scored; top 2% (98th percentile) typically flagged as HIN
- **Equity overlay:** correlate HIN with low-income, minority, LEP, disabled, youth, elderly populations
- **Output:** prioritized corridors for engineering investment

Sources: [Vision Zero HIN Methodology (Boston MPO 2026)](https://bostonmpo.org/data/html/plans/Vision-Zero/2026/Vision%20Zero%20App%20C%20High-Injury%20Network%20Methodology.html), [SF Vision Zero 2022 Methodology](https://www.visionzerosf.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/2022_Vision_Zero_Network_Update_Methodology.pdf).

## 9. Ride-hailing specific research

This is where this project has the most defensible novelty.

**Net safety impact of TNCs (San Francisco study):**

- TNCs indirectly increased total crashes by 4% (more vehicle exposure)
- Plus 7% more crashes from speed changes
- Direct TNC effect: -14% crashes
- Net: small contributor; the mechanism (curb conflicts, exposure) matters more than the headline

**Curb design findings:**

- Angled stall designs are safer when passengers wait longer (recommend passengers approach only after vehicle arrives)
- Curbside designs are more efficient (shorter dwell) but more conflict-prone
- Seattle: expanded Passenger Load Zones plus geofencing reduced travel-lane stopping and dwell time, but did not measurably change crash rates in the study window

**Pedestrian and accessibility harms:**

- TNC pickup/dropoff blocks pedestrian paths, bike lanes, and ADA-compliant curb ramps. This is a documented Vision Zero equity issue.

Sources: [Ridesharing and motor vehicle crashes (BMJ Injury Prevention)](https://injuryprevention.bmj.com/content/27/2/118), [Testing Curbside Management Strategies (Urban Freight Lab)](https://urbanfreightlab.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Testing-Curbside-to-Mitigate.pdf), [TNC PUDO Simulator/Microsimulation (TRB)](https://pubsindex.trb.org/view/2024/C/2221118), [Managing the Curb (BTS)](https://rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/63268/dot_63268_DS1.pdf).

## 10. Cross-organization synthesis - shared priorities

Every organization above converges on the same short list. Use this as the spine of any briefing notes.

| Theme | Toronto | VZN | USDOT | FHWA | NACTO | WHO | iRAP |
|-------|:-------:|:---:|:-----:|:----:|:-----:|:---:|:----:|
| Safe System framework | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Vulnerable road users first (pedestrian/cyclist) | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Speed management | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Data-driven prioritization (HIN-style) | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes | - | - | Yes (Star Rating) |
| Equity overlay | - | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes | - |
| Proactive/preventive design | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Post-crash care | - | - | Yes | Yes | - | Yes | - |
| Complete Streets / multimodal | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes | - |

**Five themes any Vision Zero audience will recognize and reward:**

1. Eliminate KSI, not just reduce crashes. Frame outcomes in killed/serious injury terms.
2. Protect vulnerable road users first. Pedestrian and cyclist KSI is the headline metric.
3. Safe System pillars. Every recommendation should map to Safe Roads / Safe Speeds / Safe Users / Safe Vehicles / Post-Crash Care.
4. High Injury Network logic. Prioritize the small share of corridors carrying the bulk of risk.
5. Equity overlay. Disproportionate harm in disadvantaged neighborhoods is no longer optional context.

## 11. How this maps back to the ride-hailing project

Concrete framing tweaks that will land with the Vision Zero team:

| Project component | Reframe as |
|-------------------|------------|
| Ward choropleth | Ward-scale exposure metric for the Safe Roads + Safe Speeds pillars |
| Q1 x Q1 scorecard | Curb-conflict equity flag: wards in top quartile for both ride-hail dropoffs and pedestrian KSI |
| Segment risk classifier | Proactive Road Design Audit Tool: Toronto-localized analog to iRAP Star Rating |
| Scenario planner (PUDO, protected bike) | Counterfactual intervention simulator aligned with FHWA Safe System pillars |
| PDF briefing note | One-page Council Staff Report format: Background, Current Status, Data-Driven Recommendation |

**Vocabulary that earns credibility** (use literally in the README, dashboard copy, and briefing PDF):

- "Killed or Seriously Injured (KSI)" instead of "crashes"
- "High Injury Network" instead of "hotspots"
- "Vulnerable road users" instead of "pedestrians and cyclists"
- "Safe System" instead of "Vision Zero" when describing methodology
- "Exposure-normalized" rates (TMC volumes in denominator) instead of raw counts
- "Curb conflict" instead of "double-parking"
